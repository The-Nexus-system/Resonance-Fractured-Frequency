**Original status note:**

**Format.** A self-contained episode in Star Trek pacing — cold open, four acts,
coda — delivered Halo-style: all narrative arrives in-world through voices,
places, overheard life, and the player's AI channel. No cutscene lectures, no
quiz cards, no menus that teach. People introduce mechanics (Canon Rule).

**Length.** ~60 minutes for a curious first playthrough. In-world time: two days
of travel and transfer, seven days aboard ship (the Master Timeline week, Day
Seven counting down to Day One), Attack Day, and roughly two weeks of deep-space
survival compressed into the back half.

**Player.** The player is an original professional (Perspective Map professions),
with player-chosen name, callsign, pronouns, voice, communication mode, and
accessibility profile (Player Identity doc). Nothing in this script assumes
sight, hearing, or speech; every beat has an acoustic identity and an
accessibility-equivalent channel.

**Resonance moments.** Four earned reward events, tied to accomplishments, never
to hunting objects. Each is a short musical event that blooms and then resolves
back into ordinary world audio — never a continuous chord.
- Moment 1: **F** alone.
- Moment 2: **F + A**.
- Moment 3: **F + A + C**.
- Moment 4: **F + A + C + E** — the complete FACE sonority. (House rule: it is
  called the FACE sonority, never "F major". The final note is E.)
Each moment also lands as haptic pattern + captioned description + visible light
bloom, per the Audio Bible's equivalence rules.

**Scoring and inventory.** Points are Standing (community regard, per the
"Reputation Is Earned Through Contribution" rule). Inventory items are physical,
diegetic objects with acoustic identities. Both persist into later chapters.

# COLD OPEN — "The Last Quiet of Earth" (0:00–0:04)

**Place.** Passenger cabin of the transfer shuttle *Long Answer* (name TBD —
gap), final hours of the Earth–Moon crossing. The player has a couch, a window
(or window-tone for nonvisual players: a soft filtered hiss that is Earthlight),
and a **comm badge** clipped at the collar — the standard loaner badge every
traveller carries until they choose a permanent channel.

**How guidance works in this act.** The badge is polite, external, public — a
tannoy shrunk to a lapel. It speaks in short, practical lines. It is
deliberately NOT intimate; the contrast with the Act One choice is the point.

**Environmental storytelling.** Other passengers: a family re-securing a cello
case; two workers asleep against each other; someone practising sign against
the window's reflection. No exposition. The cello case will be heard again on
Hearth (Day Five) — planted here.

BADGE: Crossing complete in forty minutes. Lunar approach is loud.
       If you would like the quiet couches, they are aft.

**Player verbs (tutorialised by doing, not telling):** move, orient, listen,
touch/inspect, speak or sign or type to the badge (any input mode answers).

**Optional overheard #1** (sit near the family):
PARENT: ...it's not that they won't have music there. It's that this one
        came from your grandmother's house. Some things should arrive
        with somebody, not in a crate.

**Why the player left Earth — handled without canon invention.** Canon defines
no personal motive (Universe Bible). The script keeps it player-authored: the
badge asks one gentle intake question, and the player picks a reason-shape that
colours later dialogue but is never narrated back at them as fixed backstory.

BADGE: Arrival intake asks one question and you may decline it.
       What are you travelling toward?
CHOICE (flavour only, no score):
  A) "Work I couldn't do down there."
  B) "People. Or the hope of them."
  C) "Away. That's enough for now."
  D) (Decline.) — BADGE: Declined. Plenty arrive that way too.

**Trigger to Act One:** docking clamps. A deep triple knock — the first sound
of the episode's percussion motif, which returns at the battle.

# ACT ONE — "Waystation" (0:04–0:12)

## Scene 1.1 — Lunar station concourse

**Place.** LUNAR TRANSFER STATION (name TBD — gap). Purpose per this script:
the ordinary interchange where Earth shuttles meet outbound civilisation ships.
It is deliberately unremarkable — a bus station, not a monument — because the
episode's wonder is saved for Hearth.

**Guide.** SOLACE, the station's public voice, takes over from the badge the
moment the player steps off the ramp. Same badge, different voice: warmer,
older, unhurried.

SOLACE: Welcome to the waystation. You're listed for the CSV Hearth,
        departing in eleven hours, station time. That's enough time
        to eat, to walk, and to make one decision that is yours alone.
        No part of it needs to be rushed. The floor lines hum —
        follow the low one to food, the high one to the Hearth berth.

**Mechanics taught here:** guidance lines with distinct acoustic identities
(low hum = amenities, high hum = departure), Ping-style orientation on request,
inventory (first item below).

**Inventory item 1 — the loaner badge itself.** SOLACE mentions, in passing,
that departing travellers may keep their badge: "Most people keep the first
one. It's the object that heard you arrive." (+1 keepsake item: *First Badge*.)

**Environmental storytelling / station legacy (canon rule in action):** a wall
of repairs left visible, each patch stamped with a year and a first name.
Inspecting any patch gives one line of the station's legacy — who fixed it,
what it survived. Three patches reference the Chain of Rescue obliquely
("Patched by Ondine, who arrived with nothing and left this").

**Optional overheard #2** (concourse benches, two Second Chance Fleet crew):
CREW A: Three retrievals this rotation. The last one wouldn't believe
        we weren't charging her anything.
CREW B: Nobody believes it the first time. That's why there's a
        second time.
(This is the episode's only explicit Retrieval Network/Second Chance Fleet
reference before the back half — planted, not lectured.)

## Scene 1.2 — The intake clinic: THE FIRST MEANINGFUL CHOICE

**Place.** A small, acoustically soft clinic room off the concourse.
**Character.** Dr. Ash Idowu, intake clinician. Unhurried. Presents both
options without steering, per the Professional Neutrality canon rule.

IDOWU: Aboard Hearth you'll have a channel — the ship's voice, your
       lessons, and one day, if you want it, a companion intelligence
       of your own. The channel is yours to shape. Two ways to carry it.

IDOWU: A neural interface. Worn here — (touch at the temple, a click) —
       comes off whenever you like. You'll feel it as equipment,
       because it is. Reversible today, reversible always.

IDOWU: Or a neural implant. Inside, and long-term. Richer. Closer.
       Some people describe it as the difference between hearing a
       voice in a room and having a thought arrive already warm.
       It asks more of you first — counselling, consent that we
       check twice, and time. We'd finish the process aboard Hearth.

IDOWU: Neither is braver. Neither is more committed. And whichever
       you choose, you may stand in a room like this one someday
       and choose again.

**CHOICE 2 (permanent for this campaign; both fully viable):**
- **INTERFACE.** Immediate. The channel voice sits slightly "outside" — mixed
  with room reverb, positioned at the shoulder. Item: *Interface Band* (can be
  physically removed in play for true silence — a mechanic, not a punishment).
- **IMPLANT.** Consent process runs as a quiet scene (two verbal confirmations
  separated by a walk to the berth — canon-consistent graduated consent, the
  final activation happening on Day Six aboard). The channel voice is mixed
  dry, interior, centred. Item: *Consent Record* (a keepsake copy, "because
  the promise belongs to you, not the clinic").

**Consequences:** texture only — where the AI voice sits, privacy of replies
(implant players may subvocalise; interface players' replies are soft-spoken
or signed or typed), and a handful of dialogue variations. No stat advantage.
Faience, much later, inherits this same channel.

## Scene 1.3 — Boarding

The high floor-line. The Hearth berth opens from a service corridor —
deliberately mundane — and then the hull. First sound of the Hearth: a chord
of ventilation, voices, and somewhere a cello being tuned. The badge hands
the player off:

SOLACE: This is where my voice ends. Hearth carries her own.
        Travel well. Be somebody's story about a good arrival.

HEARTH VOICE (through the player's chosen channel, first line):
        Boarding acknowledged. Welcome aboard, {callsign}.
        Deck Four, cabin forty-one. Your First Hand is already
        looking for you.

**Trigger to Act Two:** crossing the airlock threshold. The Master Timeline
week begins: this is the evening of **Day Seven**.

# ACT TWO — "A Week of Ordinary Wonders" (0:12–0:32)

The seven in-world days (Day Seven counting down to Day One, per the Master
Timeline) compress into ~20 playable minutes. Each day is a short scene with
one anchor activity, one optional overheard conversation, and one free verb.
The Vale System's morning announcement opens each day (canon: the morning
announcements), fronted by whichever captain persona the Master Timeline
assigns that day — the player hears them but is never singled out by them.

**Design rule for the whole act:** show the culture, never lecture it. Every
"cultural lesson" is a person doing something and letting the player join.

## Day Seven (evening) — Cabin, and the First Hand

Cabin forty-one: small, warm, a bunk that hums faintly at F when touched —
the ship's structural tone (planted; pays off in every resonance moment).

**Brother-Sister Wren** (First Hand; works in the infirmary; trauma-medicine
lineage — they trained at the medical station the episode ends at):

WREN: Found you. I'm Wren — Brother-Sister Wren if you want the long
      of it. I'm your First Hand, which means two things. One: I teach
      you your work. Two: I make sure the work never becomes the whole
      of your life. Most people only expect the first thing.
      Hungry? Good. Rule one of the Hearth: nobody learns anything
      on an empty stomach. Come on. Food's this way, and so is
      everyone worth meeting.

**Anchor: the mess hall.** Learn-where-food-is as an actual scene: trays,
the serving rhythm, where you sit matters socially. Sitting at any occupied
table opens listening; staying long enough opens joining.

**Optional overheard #3 (mess, Day Seven):** two engineers arguing joyfully
about whether a repair should be hidden or left visible ("A hull that hides
its scars is lying about what it's survived") — station-legacy culture, aboard
a ship.

**Score:** +Standing for sitting with strangers (small, invisible until later).

## Day Six — Work, and the first note

**Anchor: the player's first real task**, given by Wren, matched to the chosen
profession (eight variants, one per Perspective Map profession — each a
concrete, physical, ~90-second job with real ship consequence: a valve
rebalanced, a sensor recalibrated, a meal batch rescued, a child's AAC rig
retuned...). The channel (Hearth Voice) provides Halo-style contextual help —
short lines triggered by what the player is touching, never a tutorial box.

(Implant players: the second consent confirmation and activation happens this
morning — a ten-second scene, Idowu's voice on comm, Wren present as witness.)

**RESONANCE MOMENT 1 — F.** Triggered the moment the task genuinely completes.
The ship's structural F swells out of the bunk-hum timbre, blooms for ~6
seconds — one note, warm, enormous — then resolves back into ordinary room
tone. Haptic: one long soft pulse. Caption: "The ship's own note, answering
work well done."

WREN (quietly, not explaining it away): Heard that, did you? She does
      that. Old ships hum for good work. Ask three people why and
      you'll get four stories.

**Inventory:** profession tool earned (persistent; e.g. *Wren's Spare Probe*).

## Day Five — Culture by participation

**Anchor: a knowledge-exchange circle** in the arboretum ring — the canon
"every person arrives with knowledge" rule played straight. The circle asks
arrivals to teach one small thing. The player chooses what to offer:
  A) something from their profession,
  B) something from Earth (a song fragment, a recipe, a word),
  C) to listen this time (also honoured: "Listening is a contribution.
     Somebody has to carry it forward.")
The cello from the cold open is here; the family plays two minutes of
something old. Mira (Chapter One canon, AAC user) asks the player a question
through her interface — the first time the player's own channel translates
another person's channel. No comment is made about it. That IS the lesson.

**Optional overheard #4:** two elders on the Chain of Rescue, told as family
history, not doctrine ("Her ship found mine. Mine found two more. You don't
pay that back. You pay it forward. That's the whole religion of it.")

## Day Four — The ship's body

**Anchor: layout mastery.** Wren sends the player on a real errand across four
decks (deliver infirmary supplies), teaching routes, the lift's voice, section
door chimes (each deck has a pitch — deck chimes quietly outline F, A, C, E
across the ship; never stated, just true). Free exploration unlocked from here.

**Practical choice:** a corridor is half-blocked by a crew team mid-repair.
Squeeze past (+time) or reroute and carry a message for them (+Standing,
+overheard #5: what the anomaly readings have been doing all week — the Master
Timeline's anomaly thread, present but unexplained, as canon requires).

## Day Three — The second note

**Anchor: something goes slightly wrong** — a genuine but small emergency
matched to profession (a pressure drift, a panicking passenger, a failed
batch, a jammed pod hatch during a routine drill — the drill plants pod
mechanics for Act Three). The player handles it with one other crew member,
using Day Six's tool.

**RESONANCE MOMENT 2 — F + A.** On resolution: the structural F returns and,
above it, an A joins — a third, an interval, the first harmony. ~8 seconds,
then back to room tone. Haptic: two pulses, low then higher. Caption: "A
second note joins the ship's. An interval: two things, agreeing."

**Score/inventory:** +Standing; item *Drill Token* (pod-bay access chip —
"everyone who's drilled carries one" — quietly load-bearing for Act Three).

## Day Two — Rest is also canon

**Anchor: a social evening.** Music in the mess; the player may sit, request
something, dance, help serve, or step out to the observation blister where
Wren tells one story of the medical station they trained at:

WREN: There's a station out past the relay shallows that's better at
      trauma medicine than anywhere humans have ever been. Not because
      of machines. Because for three generations, everyone who was
      put back together there stayed to put someone else back
      together. You can hear it in the place. Corridors full of
      people who know exactly how bad it can get, being gentle.
(The destination, planted as a place the player will want to reach.)

**Optional overheard #6:** two crew debating implants versus interfaces as
casually as glasses versus contacts — bodily-autonomy canon shown as small
talk, echoing the player's own Act One choice back at them without judgement.

## Day One — The third note, and getting comfortable

**Anchor: the player's work is trusted.** Wren hands them a task WITHOUT
supervision for the first time — and a small ceremony follows: the section
adds the player's first name to a visible repair patch (station-legacy
tradition, now including them).

**RESONANCE MOMENT 3 — F + A + C.** As the name goes on: F, A, and now C —
a full triad, ~10 seconds, voices in the mess audibly pausing for it, then
ordinary sound returns. Haptic: three ascending pulses. Caption: "Three notes
now. It is beginning to sound like a place you live."

HEARTH VOICE: For what it is worth, {callsign} — and it is worth
      a great deal — the ship sounds better with you aboard.

**This is the top of the arc. The player is comfortable. Hold the warmth for
sixty full seconds of free play. Then:**

**Trigger to Act Three:** the morning announcement of Attack Day begins —
and cuts off mid-word. The triple docking-knock percussion from the cold open
returns, wrong: faster, hull-borne, from outside.

# ACT THREE — "Attack Day" (0:32–0:42)

Attack Day is Founding Day (Master Timeline canon: the attack begins the
community's history). The act runs ~10 minutes: two of battle, eight of
evacuation and branch.

## Scene 3.1 — The battle (0:32–0:34)

The player never sees the Adversary. The battle is experienced from inside a
civilian ship: impacts as position-specific hull tones, the Vale System's
voice changing fronts mid-crisis (canon: fronting is gameplay — Naomi takes
emergency command from whichever persona opened the day, per the Master
Timeline's Attack Day fronting, while Iris carries the civilian channels),
crew doing their jobs around the player. The channel goes terse:

HEARTH VOICE: {callsign}. This is real. Deck Four is holding.
      Stay with Wren. I will not be able to speak much.

VALE (NAOMI, all-ship command; IRIS echoing on the civilian
channels): All residents to evacuation stations.
      Children to pod rows first. Walk. Help the person beside you.
      We practised this. You are all better at it than you believe.

**No combat verbs.** The player's verbs are the week's verbs — move, carry,
help — under pressure. Everything Act Two taught (routes, deck chimes, the
drill token, names of people) is now the gameplay.

## Scene 3.2 — The pod row and THE BRANCH (0:34–0:40)

Wren and the player reach pod row Delta. Wren is triaging arrivals and will
NOT board this pod ("First Hands load last. That's the whole job, in the
end."). The pod's manifest lists **six survivors** still inbound (a
predetermined list, seeded from people met in Act Two — the cello family,
Mira, an engineer from overheard #3, one profession-specific colleague).

HEARTH VOICE: Pod Delta-Nine is yours. Six more are coming to it.
      You can hold the row and bring them in — or board now and
      run the launch sequence so it is ready the moment they arrive.
      Both are right. Choose.

**BRANCH A — HOLD THE ROW ("wait outside, gathering").**
Playable: ~4 minutes of finding/guiding each listed survivor through smoke
and noise (each is a micro-scene using an Act Two relationship).
- Score: **+6 Standing** (one per survivor personally brought in).
- Inventory gained: *Mira's Spare Interface Clip*, *the Cello* (the family
  cannot carry it and the player can — it becomes the improvised ship's
  cultural object), *Wren's Field Kit* (pressed into the player's hands:
  "You'll need this more than me. Go.").
- Cost: the launch sequence is unstarted; the launch scene is rougher —
  one system (player's choice: lights or gravity assist) fails uncalibrated
  and stays degraded until repaired in Act Four.

**BRANCH B — DIVE IN ("start the running sequence").**
Playable: ~4 minutes inside the pod: power-up, seat checks, seal checks,
calling out readiness as each survivor boards themselves — heard, not
gathered. The drill token from Day Three speeds every step.
- Score: **+2 Standing** (crews credit the clean launch).
- Inventory gained: *Pod Master Key* (full system access earned by running
  the sequence), *Calibration Log* (Act Four repairs run faster), one seat's
  emergency ration cache.
- Cost: the cello is left behind (the family grieves it in Act Four — one
  quiet scene, no blame); Mira boards shaken, and one listed survivor
  arrives injured (they boarded unassisted) — Act Four's first patient.

**MERGE:** both branches end in the same launch: all six survivors aboard,
same core cast, same story spine. Only Standing, inventory, one degraded
system, and several relationship textures differ.

## Scene 3.3 — Launch (0:40–0:42)

Violent acceleration, the tearing metal of Chapter Two canon — now with a
full pod, not alone. Then, exactly as canon requires: quiet, disorientation,
no interface. The Hearth Voice is gone. The channel is silent for the first
time since Act One. Hold the silence (Canon Rule: silence is deliberate
storytelling) for twenty full seconds.

Then, through the player's chosen channel — interior for implant players,
at-the-shoulder for interface players — something that is not the Hearth:

FAIENCE (first words, faint, unformed): ...someone is listening.
      Good. I was not sure I was someone yet.

(Canon preserved: Faience first stirs in the player's escape pod. The Act One
choice now pays off as the texture of the whole relationship.)

# ACT FOUR — "The Ship You Make" (0:42–0:56)

Deep space. ~14 minutes. Structure: three survival beats, then the
pod-linking build, then the fourth note. Chapter Two's canon systems —
stabilise, beacon, search signals, most signals silent or corrupted, no
automatic navigation — are all here, but crewed: the survivors are hands,
voices, and needs, not cargo.

## Beat 4.1 — Run silent, run dark (evasion)

Faience (growing more articulate scene by scene) reads the sky: attacker
sweeps are audible as slow radar-brush tones. Navigation is the player
physically steering AWAY from brushes — continuous input, no buttons-as-quiz.
Branch A players do this with one degraded system; Branch B players with the
Calibration Log do it faster.

**Branch A repair beat (closes the degraded-system loop):** once the last
sweep fades, Wren's Field Kit plus one survivor's hands (the engineer from
overheard #3, if gathered) restore the degraded system in a short two-person
repair scene — the episode's quiet thesis, spoken by the engineer: "Everything
on this boat got fixed by somebody once." If the player skips it, Faience
re-offers it during Beat 4.4, and the system is restored automatically when
the pods merge power (linked ships share strength); the repair scene is the
richer path, not a gate.

## Beat 4.2 — Triage

The injured survivor (Branch B) or an injury sustained during launch
(Branch A — lighter) is treated using Wren's Field Kit or the pod's minimal
kit. Trauma-medicine culture, shown: the survivors self-organise around the
patient the way Wren's people would. Someone says, unprompted:

SURVIVOR: There's a station for this. The one Wren trained at.
      If we're going anywhere, we're going there.
(The destination is now chosen BY the community, not assigned by a quest log.)

## Beat 4.3 — Life support arithmetic

One honest resource scene: air, heat, water for eight people in a six-berth
pod. The player makes two allocation choices with real but survivable
consequences. No death spirals; this is a culture that does not punish care.

## Beat 4.4 — The Chain of Rescue, from the other side

The beacon (Chapter Two canon) goes out. First contact back is ROWAN's
corrupted maintenance beacon — canon's "...Maintenance... ...Rowan...
...Anybody..." — and the player must physically reach him: the pod-linking
sequence begins. Docking is manual, tactile, audio-guided by Faience
(alignment tones converging — the interval training of Moment 2, now a
mechanic). Rowan's pod hard-links. Two people, two pods, one shared air
system. Then a second contact: a pod of strangers. Then a third — found not
by the player but BY a Second Chance Fleet scout too small to carry anyone,
which instead relays positions ("We can't lift you. But we can make sure
everyone knows exactly where you are. That's most of rescue, some days.")

Each linked pod is a build choice: its systems can be merged as
**power**, **medical**, **quarters**, or **drive** — the improvised ship
takes shape according to the player's sequence. The cello (Branch A) is
mounted in the junction node; someone plays it softly during the last link.

## RESONANCE MOMENT 4 — F + A + C + E (the FACE sonority)

Triggered when the final pod locks and the linked structure fires its
engines AS ONE SHIP for the first time. F from the old Hearth structural
tone (the pods carry it in their bones), A and C returning, and then —
new, at the top, unheard in the whole episode — **E**. The complete FACE
sonority, ~12 seconds, every hull ringing, then resolving back into the
ordinary creak-and-hum of a ship that is now theirs. Haptic: four pulses,
the last one long. Caption: "Four notes. The last one is new — like the
ship. Like all of you, together."

FAIENCE: I have been listening for a name for what you have built.
      I think the ship just suggested one. We can decide later.
(The improvised ship's name is left for the player/creator — deliberate gap.)

# ACT FIVE / CODA — "The Station Known for Mending" (0:56–1:00)

The medical station (name TBD — gap) resolves out of the dark the way the
episode taught the player to perceive everything: sound first — a wide,
layered soundscape of many communities' voices — then light. Approach and
docking use the pod-linking skill one final time, at civilised scale, with a
station traffic voice that speaks like SOLACE's older sibling.

STATION VOICE: Improvised vessel, eight souls and linked pods, we
      have you. You're the third self-made ship this season and the
      best-sounding one yet. Medical teams are at the ring.
      You're done flying alone. Come in.

Docking clamps: the same triple knock from the cold open — right, this time.
Standing total is spoken as community welcome, not a score screen (high
Standing: named survivors tell the receiving crew what the player did;
lower Standing: the welcome is no less warm, the stories just fewer).
Final inventory is carried off visibly in-hand.

Last beat: Wren's fate is deliberately unresolved this episode (they loaded
last; no confirmation either way) — the hook for the next chapter, honest to
canon's rule that history and understanding are earned separately.

FAIENCE (final line): Earth is a long way behind you. I checked.
      But I do not think "behind" is the direction that matters
      to you anymore. Welcome home, {callsign}. The first one.

Fade on the station soundscape absorbing the ship's F into its own chord.

**END OF EPISODE ONE.**

# Systems appendix (for implementation)

- **No quiz cards anywhere.** All four legacy puzzle nodes are retired.
  Resonance moments are earned by: first completed profession task (F),
  first crisis resolved (F+A), first trusted solo work / naming ceremony
  (F+A+C), first flight of the linked ship (F+A+C+E).
- **Notes:** F–A–C–E cumulative, reward events only, always resolving back
  to world audio. Never a continuous chord. Never called "F major"; the
  sonority is FACE and the final note is E. (Supersedes the code's current
