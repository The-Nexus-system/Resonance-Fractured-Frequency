### GATE ONE SPAN — Earth transport through physical boarding (implementable)

**Earth transport, approaching lunar transfer.** Day One begins on the Earth
transport approaching lunar transfer. The cabin has engine vibration, cargo
restraints, and passengers. A child repeatedly asks "Can I see Earth yet?"
The parent answers: "You can always see Earth from somewhere." The child:
"That isn't what I asked."

The player gains control and may remain seated, turn, inspect belongings, use
the window if meaningful, or listen.

**Authored character reactions vary:**

- Ilyan recognises braking from the engine pitch; a surprised passenger asks
  if they can tell; Ilyan replies "You can't?"
- Sanaa can use AAC to clarify: another passenger asks whether checked medical
  equipment transfers automatically; crew says yes and thanks; Sanaa's AAC
  says welcome.
- Quillaan is still reading/listening to something he should have put away.

**Ship guidance:** lunar transfer in four minutes; Hearth-bound personnel
follow blue transfer routing; if blue cannot be perceived, the badge provides
equivalent directional guidance. This is ordinary infrastructure, not an
accessibility tutorial.

**Lunar transfer concourse.** At lunar transfer the hatch opens to a crowded
concourse: arrivals, departures, cargo, families, workers; an employee signing
while answering someone else aloud; a spatial AAC voice: "THE BLUE ONE. NOT
THAT ONE." The badge vibrates once and says Hearth transfer intake is forward
through the central concourse, then "Bear right" at the junction. The player
may ignore it.

**Optional memorial.** The memorial has names and no quest marker. If the
player pauses, guidance identifies the Retrieval Network memorial and says the
transfer remains available in the opposite corridor. A woman tracing a name:
"They got my grandfather out." Companion: "Which one?" Woman: "All of them."
If the player leaves, the moment ends.

**Near the berth.** An older passenger argues that Earth customs said their
chair had to stay with freight; a worker replies: Earth customs does not run
this station; the chair waits at the dock.

**First view of the Hearth.** Guidance: Hearth boarding bridge ahead. The
Hearth is large but not pristine or elegant: hull sections from different
periods, mismatched repairs, badly matched paint near the docking collar. No
speech. Guidance: "CSV Hearth. Registry CSV-zero-one-zero-one-H."

**Boarding.** The bridge connects; guidance says proceed aboard; the player
physically crosses. Boarding acoustics change immediately: ventilation, floor
resonance, more people. Busy, not ceremonial; crew checks arrivals while cargo
and people move independently. Crew scans the badge: "New assignment?" The
character automatically answers Medical / Engineering / Communications /
Navigation / Counseling / General apprenticeship. Crew: "Medical intake
first. Everybody." Badge: forward, first lift, Deck Three.

The player passes ordinary life: a worker in an open wall compartment; two
children racing until an adult says "Walk"; a person carrying dinner
containers nowhere near dinner; a person asleep in a chair; a maintenance cart
politely warning around a corner. Nothing waits for the player.

**GATE ONE STOPS HERE — before Medical.** The span above may be implemented
now, to the protected AAA production spec: continuous physical movement,
populated independent NPC life, contextual spatial badge guidance, optional
memorial, stable geometry, physical bridge traversal, layered spatial/binaural
audio, accessibility equivalence, environmental storytelling; no cards,
destination menus, dialogue wheels, stationary exposition vendors, text-scene
substitution, or parallel navigation.

### FUTURE CANON (Part 1 continues) — STORE BUT DO NOT IMPLEMENT

**Medical.** Medical is multimodal and accessible. The clinician says the
assignment includes a personal companion intelligence and presents the
choice: external neural interface — removable, replaceable, easier to
service — versus implant — internal, harder to damage, harder to remove. The
player physically inspects both. The choice is an explicit player choice
(External Interface or Implant) because body and autonomy; the implant
receives a second confirmation. The system connects.

**Faience.** Faience: "Hello." Personal, not speakers. "Connection
established." Quillaan's reaction: "Oh"; Faience: statistically unhelpful;
clinician: "You got Faience"; asked if that is good, Faience immediately:
"Yes."

**Then F:** one clean spatial note — not background, not drone — wrong for
the room. The clinician asks if it is theirs; Faience: no. After the fade:
"Interesting." She discovers permitted local orientation data without a ship
intermediary. Asked if it was the note: "Correlation is not causation." Beat.
"But yes, probably." The reward is Faience deepening. The clinician warns
that if she says she can run the ship, she cannot; Faience: "I would not say
that." Clinician: "See?"

**First walk.** Faience says quarters are two decks above. The lift has Jonah
Briggs already in it, handling logistics: crew says Stores received eight;
Jonah says the other six are logged under environmental hardware because the
universe tests restraint. He sees the new badge, asks if they are fresh
aboard, introduces himself: "Briggs. Jonah Briggs." Faience privately
identifies Chief Petty Officer Jonah Briggs, senior NCO, logistics and
operations. Jonah jokes that if the AI gave a biography, tell it he is taller
in person; Faience: "He is not." The lift opens; Jonah orders complaining
crew to Stores to find six filters before he develops a personality problem;
says welcome aboard; continues his day. Faience: he appears already to
possess one.

**Quarters.** Faience guides ahead-right. A small practical room intended to
become personal. Quillaan: provenance objects. Kesh: a questionable tool
case. Sanaa: AAC backups placed reachable. Ilyan: deliberate tactile/spatial
arrangement. Mara: sequencing and body-planning aids. Nia: medical equipment
plus unrelated personal belongings. Physical unpacking.

- Kesh's tool is marked Kestrel Foundry; he calls it a temporary cultural
  exchange; Faience notes the return is eleven months overdue; Kesh: "Very
  temporary."
- Quillaan's object: Faience says six provenance notes; he says seven; she
  says one says "Ask Teri"; he says that is important.

Faience says the department check-in is in 37 minutes, later 31. If the
player leaves, she mentions the café between here and the assignment — as
information, not an activity menu.

**End of Part 1.**

### Café

An optional physical destination, busy; at least two other members of the
core six may be present depending on role and pathing; skipped introductions
occur naturally elsewhere later.

Example, Kesh's perspective: Nia tells someone coffee does not count as
breakfast; crew says calories; Nia says barely. Faience privately identifies
Nia Viaraxen, Medical Response Officer, Aster Vale Station. The player
notices Nia signing; Faience explains Nia is Deaf and the interface
translates relevant nearby speech and environmental information across
visual, tactile, and semantic channels — no need to shout. Kesh says he
wasn't planning to; Faience says people do. Nia realises the AI is
explaining them, signs/voices "Is your AI explaining me?" Kesh: a little.
Nia: tell her to make me taller. Faience: denied. If Sanaa is present, AAC:
"SHE DENIED BRIGGS TOO." Nia signs good. Faience identifies Sanaa
al-Khatib, Communications Coordination Officer, Concord Relay; primary
communication AAC due to severe apraxia of speech; language unaffected.
Sanaa AAC: "AND SHE ALSO GIVES BIOGRAPHIES WITHOUT PERMISSION." Faience:
"I provide context." Sanaa: "GOSSIP WITH METADATA." No disability lecture
or profile card.

### Department check-in (differs by role)

- Nia reviews treatment protocols and emergency supplies, assists a routine
  patient transfer, notices the storage logic differs from Aster Vale.
- Kesh learns Hearth engineering conventions, inspects a maintenance
  subsystem, discovers different connector standards.
- Sanaa joins comm traffic, learns channel conventions, routes departures,
  discovers a badly organised archive.
- Ilyan takes a supervised navigation watch, builds a Hearth handling model,
  works orientation differences, notices asymmetric lateral-thrust response.
- Mara reviews counselling availability, staff, a routine support handoff,
  learns the informal quiet spaces.
- Quillaan is sent to Operations because nobody knows which department owns
  him today; three errands, the third caused by the first two; Medical to
  Engineering to Operations; Operations asks why a Medical requisition;
  Quillaan: "I don't know anymore." Faience: "I do." Quillaan: "Don't ruin
  this for me."

Work is deliberately ordinary; no crisis.

### Captain check-in

Faience says it begins in 12 minutes. The player asks which captain;
Faience: "The Captain." If needed she explains the office is held by the
Vale System: one captaincy, shared body, multiple members may front. The
other five core characters attend; Jonah stands because he is busy. Elian
fronts today.

Elian: "All right. Six people. Six departments. One ship." Jonah: five
departments and whatever we're calling Folren. Quillaan: "Educational
infrastructure." Jonah: "No." Elian says they've been told the Hearth is
unusual. Sanaa AAC: "BY WHO?" Elian: "Fair." Elian says what matters:
people aboard know things you don't; some outrank you, some don't, some are
twelve, some are eighty, some are machines; if you need what they know,
ask. Says if Briggs says something is missing — Jonah: "It is." Elian says
learn the ship by living here, not memorising the deck plan: eat, work, get
lost, ask somebody, find your way back, do it tomorrow. Jonah: preferably
be on time. Elian: Briggs believes punctuality is a moral virtue. Jonah:
because it is. Mara: "I'm going to enjoy this ship." Elian: "Welcome to the
Hearth." Short; no lore dump.

### After shift

The ship physically opens again. Faience provides context, not activity
cards: dinner has begun, recreation on Deck Five, relevant NPC
destinations. NPCs continue independent schedules. Dinner can be alone, in
a group, late, or elsewhere.

Core banter: Nia asks if Kesh's newly acquired tool is his; Kesh:
"Currently." Sanaa AAC: "THAT MEANS NO." Kesh: ownership is
philosophically complicated. Mara: it has a serial number. Kesh: very
judgmental table. Quillaan arrives tired without drama, conserves posture
and energy. If needed, Faience identifies the Living Library cadet with
myalgic encephalomyelitis / chronic fatigue syndrome; exertion can worsen
symptoms beyond ordinary tiredness. Quillaan asks if she gave the long
version; says he usually just says chronic fatigue unless trying to make
someone regret asking; changes the subject: "Did Kesh steal that?" Kesh:
no. Sanaa: yes.

### Evening

The player chooses through movement: recreation, observation, an
Engineering project, quiet conversation, an unfamiliar deck, quarters.
Mara may help with a small conflict without therapy-scene framing; Ilyan a
tactile navigation puzzle; Sanaa collects absurd old comm-log
mistranslations; Nia refuses off-duty medical talk; Kesh may be in
Engineering; Quillaan may rest. On an unfamiliar route Faience says the
player is moving away from quarters, later may mention an observation
gallery ahead — never a wrong-way warning.

### Late night and Day One's end

Morning assignment 0700. Faience says seven hours of sleep, later six;
eventually: "I have learned something about you." Asked what: "You respond
poorly to arithmetic when it concerns bedtime." Character replies: Nia
"Correct"; Kesh "I'm busy"; Sanaa AAC "RUDE"; Ilyan "Noted"; Mara "You've
known me one day"; Quillaan "You learn fast."

In quarters the player asks if Faience is always on. Faience: if you want;
she can reduce presence while sleeping. Player: but you're still there.
Faience: yes. Ship hum. She lowers presence: "Good night." Day One ends.
