# Episode One — Locked Resonance Progression and Chapter Rules

**Status: LOCKED CANON DIRECTIVE (Nexus, 9 August 2026).** These rules are
finalised and bind Episode One and the chapter structure going forward. Where
they conflict with the existing episode script
(`first_fracture_episode_one_script.md`), THESE RULES WIN. The full Episode

## The four resonance triggers (locked)

Resonance notes are earned at these moments and no others. Each is a short
reward event that resolves back into ordinary world audio — never a
continuous chord.

**F — Coming aboard, coming online.**
F sounds when the player boards the campaign ship, having chosen the neural
interface or the implant, and **Faience comes online with the player**. The
note marks the beginning of the partnership: a ship, a person, and a
just-woken intelligence, all starting at once.
*(Canon amendment: this supersedes the earlier seam that Faience first stirs
in the escape pod. Faience is now online from boarding, through the chosen
channel, and grows across the episode.)*

**A — The dark day.**
A is earned **only** during the major shipwide power-failure event on
**Day Four**. No other accomplishment can produce it. The power failure is a
named major beat of Day Four, lived through inside an otherwise full day
(see the day-structure rule below).

**C — The ship you make.**
C is earned **only** once the escape-pod phase has progressed far enough
that **multiple pods are physically connected and functioning together as a
real improvised ship** — shared power, shared air, shared purpose. Linking
one pod is survival; C sounds when the linked structure has genuinely become
a vessel.

**E — Arrival, and the lock.**
E is earned **only** after all of the following, in order: the improvised
ship reaches the medical station; docks correctly; the survivors disembark;
and the player character is then **helped off onto the station** — received
by the Chain of Rescue rather than walking off alone.
At E, **Faience permanently locks to that player** and begins a deeper,
permanent integration. This bond is not reversible and not transferable.
The complete four-note resolution — the FACE sonority (technically an Fmaj7;
the house name remains "the FACE sonority") — sounds **only** at this
end-of-chapter moment. Nothing earlier in the episode may play all four
notes together.

## Faience and the notes

- Faience **acknowledges every resonance moment as meaningful** — in their
  own words, in the player's chosen channel, never as a system message.
- Each stage **may unlock or improve a capability** (perception, guidance,
  translation, system reach). Capabilities are diegetic: Faience can do a
  new thing, not the menu.

## The E payoff opens Chapter Two

The E moment transitions **directly** into Chapter Two with no break in
play. Faience's line at the lock is, in spirit:

> FAIENCE: Let me show you a cool new trick.

Chapter Two then opens by revealing a **role-specific deeper integration
perk** — the first fruit of the permanent bond, matched to the player's
profession. Intended examples (final mapping to the eight canon professions
to be settled in the approved script):
- medical roles — contextual medical knowledge surfaced in the moment;
- engineering roles — engineering insight into live systems;
- communications-focused roles — richer signal support;
- navigation-focused roles — navigation integration;
- counselling/relationship-focused roles — relationship and context support;
- generalists — broad cross-role support.
*(Note: communications officer, navigator, counselor, and junior generalist
are not yet canonically named roles — see the rank/role audit. The perk list
above is the design intent; the role names await creator approval.)*

## The song-building arc

Episode One's F–A–C–E is the first chord of a longer work. Across the
chapters, the player is **slowly building a larger piece of music through
meaningful accomplishments** — each chapter contributes its own earned
notes/voicings to a growing composition, in keeping with the "Sound Is
Progression" canon rule. The full shape of that composition is defined
chapter by chapter, never front-loaded.

## Hard structural rule: every shipboard day is lived in full

For Episode One (and as the standard for shipboard play thereafter):

- **Every shipboard day must be lived through as a full day in the playable
  world**: waking, ordinary routine, meals, work shifts, NPC interactions,
  free movement, social life, personal projects, rest/sleep choices, and
  emergent events.
- **Named major plot beats happen during those days; they do not replace
  the rest of the day.** The Day Four power failure is part of Day Four,
  not the whole of it.
- **No compression.** A day may never be collapsed into a single mission,
  represented as a card, or skipped with a summary screen. Time passes by
  being played.
- **Faience and active NPCs carry story and guidance during play** — in
  keeping with "People Introduce Mechanics, Not Menus". There are no
  briefing screens standing in for lived time.
