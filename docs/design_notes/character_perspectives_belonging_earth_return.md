# Resonance — Character Perspectives, Belonging, and the Earth Return

Recorded 4 October 2026 from the “Resonance visuals” conversation with Jamal of the Nexus System. This is an authorised development record for the existing six playable characters, with approved creative direction and explicitly provisional scene proposals. It is not a replacement campaign bible or a declaration that proposed scenes are implemented.

## Status and continuity

Jamal confirmed that six playable characters already exist and that some do not come from Earth. Use their established identities, origins, professions, relationships, and devices; do not invent a replacement roster or assume all six share an Earth-exile origin. The Deaf doctor is one of the existing character concepts discussed here. No name, species, home, or language has been assigned by this note.

“Gate One”, “Gate Four”, and “Gate Nine” preserve Jamal’s terminology. They must not be silently equated with Operation One’s existing seven chapter numbers. In particular, the proposed Gate Four Earth return does not overwrite Chapter Four: Salvage. Exact placement against the existing timeline, protected opening, escape-pod assignments, and nine-gate structure needs reconciliation before scene implementation. The current request authorises preserving and connecting these ideas, not replacing established chapter scripts.

## Creative purpose confirmed by Jamal

Resonance includes battles, tender moments, and difficult experiences. Its purpose is to help people reconsider their real-world assumptions and to let someone recognise that they are not alone, have worth, and deserve dignity, love, care, and respect. Jamal’s further emphasis is essential: a person deserves a life they themselves value and consider worth living. Safety and survival are beginnings; characters need opportunities for pleasure, ambition, friendship, self-determination, and a future they want.

Reaching even one person in this way would make the work worthwhile. The game must also engage sighted and nondisabled players through its people, worlds, exploration, and meaningful action. A player’s emotional response cannot be guaranteed. These goals guide playable experiences rather than lectures.

## Gate One: design character by character

Jamal proposed giving each gate a distinct felt experience, beginning by planning Gate One separately for each of the six established characters. The resulting histories and relationships should carry through Gates Two to Nine.

For a blind character, establish what this particular person notices about the ship through their actual vision, hearing, touch, movement, smell, equipment, and experience. For a Deaf character, establish their actual hearing, language, communication, equipment, attention, and cultural experience. Neither disability is a universal sensory template. Character perspective must coexist with access for players using different modalities; essential information cannot be made inaccessible to the player for the sake of simulating a character.

For an Earth arrival, develop the specific circumstances of removal. Jamal proposed a family ultimatum: surrender the disabled family member or have the family sent to a prison mining camp. This is a possible individual backstory, not a settled origin for everyone. How the person or family was discovered remains open.

Proposed presentation: flashes of memory while awaiting the ship to the Moon, followed by time on the lunar station to encounter the place, remember what they loved, and reckon with Earth and the fact that life has changed. “Watching Earth” should be expressed appropriately for the particular character. Memories need connections to present action and progression.

Characters already living away from Earth also have their own opening experiences. The Deaf doctor receiving a new Earth arrival can learn how that person has been treated and what they expect from care. The patient encounters the doctor as a whole person with authority, expertise, and a life. Their interaction should change both characters’ understanding without reducing either to a lesson about disability.

## Proposed dog rescue and the doctor’s continuing arc

Jamal proposed that an Earth arrival had to leave their dog behind and believed nobody knew or cared. On the escape pod, they discover the Deaf doctor rescued the dog and the animal is there to hold. The doctor has been treating the patient and their animal, but withheld the dog’s presence because survival was uncertain.

The doctor’s reasoning is compassionate but flawed: perhaps it would be easier for the patient never to know than to know and watch the dog die. By Gate Nine, the doctor realises they should have said something. Presence, choice, holding the animal, and an opportunity to say goodbye may matter more to this patient than protection from knowledge. Survival and a joyful reunion do not resolve the underlying decision about withholding information.

Carry the learning into subsequent choices and relationships, rather than ending the arc at an apology. Possible expressions discussed with Kit include asking what the person wants, sharing uncertainty before the outcome is known, accepting the patient’s response without demanding forgiveness, and later allowing friends to offer the doctor care. Exact dialogue and intermediate events remain development options.

Do not resolve rescue logistics, the responsible character’s exact movements, or shared pod occupancy by overriding the established escape chronology. Reconcile this proposal against that chronology before scripting it.

## Six people becoming friends

Jamal confirmed that these experiences should draw the six playable characters into a group of friends. Friendship accumulates through consequential shared events and ordinary time together. The dog may contribute to that shared history. Care, humour, disagreement, repair, and learning each other’s communication preferences can build relationships across the gates.

People continue to belong when frightened, mistaken, unable to contribute to a battle, or simply getting through a day. Their worth is not earned through usefulness. Proposed environmental storytelling includes personal possessions, shared routines, and spaces gradually becoming homes. These are directions for later scene and asset work, not newly locked room designs.

## Proposed Gate Four Earth return

Jamal proposed that a character cast out from Earth returns with a mentor, reassured that laws have changed and are changing. Legal change does not determine the family’s response. The family may now perceive the returning person as “from up there”, unfamiliar in their technology and way of living. Some relatives disapprove, some are ecstatic, some are unsure but glad the person is safe and cared for. Preserve that range; neither uniform rejection nor instant reconciliation was requested.

Jamal corrected a proposed scene that repeated the old communication barrier: the family conversation previously would have been inaccessible, but the character’s equipment now makes it accessible. The family is taken aback when they participate. This is access through equipment, not a cure or the creation of intelligence or worth. The equipment and communication route must match the actual character.

Most importantly, Jamal required the conversation to move the game forward. It cannot function only as an emotional reunion or demonstration. The family may be discussing the character and reveal things about Earth that the returning character did not know. The newly available information must affect what the characters can investigate, decide, or do next.

## Gate Four’s campaign stakes

Jamal proposed Gate Four as work towards Earth joining space and other beings. The threat to be stopped is deliberate: someone intends to evacuate disabled, young, sick, and old people and then murder the remainder of Earth. The six and the Hearth are trying to prevent this. Preserve both parts of that threat; do not reduce it to an accidental catastrophe, an ordinary evacuation, or merely a family disagreement.

The personal return should connect directly to this wider plot. Kit proposed, and Jamal agreed to preserve the discussion, possible clues such as early departure instructions, unexplained dates, selective passenger places, or requests for information about people remaining on Earth. These are candidate mechanisms, not confirmed evidence, final perpetrators, or a fixed timetable. The family could believe they are discussing a humanitarian programme. A clue becomes an investigation rather than automatic proof.

A possible actionable interruption is a question about who supplied a departure date. The encounter might yield a message, sender, lead, deadline, or cooperation. Family relationships affect how that information is obtained. Exact clues, objectives, branches, and consequences still need designing.

Protecting people who need evacuation and preventing the murder of those remaining are simultaneous responsibilities. The threat is the perpetrators’ deliberate plan. Earth’s people do not forfeit their right to survive if political integration fails. This ethical framing supports the agreed goal of dignity and a life worth living.

## Next design work

Read the current six-character profiles and gate structure before assigning any of these beats. Establish each character’s Gate One starting point, sensory and communication experience, relationships, knowledge, meaningful actions, and changes that can carry forward. Preserve common event chronology across perspectives.

Connect the Earth-return conversation to an actionable Gate Four objective, choose the actual clue mechanism, and reconcile its place in the wider campaign. Establish the dog-rescue logistics and the doctor’s learning across gates without changing protected chronology by implication. Keep the distinction between what happens, what a character believes, and what the player has discovered.

This record preserves the discussion for campaign, character, presentation, accessibility, and future scene planning. It creates no new implementation work beyond the presently authorised build scope.
