# Episode One Authority 3: Macro Narrative and Production Spine

**Status: PROTECTED CANON — DOCUMENTATION ONLY; NO IMPLEMENTATION AUTHORIZED**

This is the third of three Episode One design authorities approved by the
creator (Nexus/Kit) on 9 August 2026. It must not be condensed, rewritten,
reinterpreted, or prematurely implemented. It defines the shape of the whole
episode so future gates are built toward it.

## F-A-C-E across the episode

The episode's musical spine is the F-A-C-E progression, earned through lived
events (per the locked trigger rules in
`first_fracture_episode_one_locked_rules.md`):

- **F** — Day One (boarding, interface/implant choice, Faience online with
  the player).
- Days Two and Three — normal life aboard the Hearth, lived in full.
- **A** — Day Four: the major Hearth power failure, with persistent
  consequences and escalating external tension thereafter.
- The attack falls on the same Hearth the player has learned; evacuation
  includes optional survivor assistance through action, not menus.
- Escape-pod survival follows.
- **C** — earned at rendezvous, through the physical construction of an
  improvised multi-pod vessel that genuinely functions as a ship.
- The run to safety ends in physical docking at the medical station:
  survivors disembark, the player is helped off.
- **E** — earned there; the deeper, permanent Faience connection forms and
  the full F-A-C-E chord resolves.
- Episode Two opens with the Faience capability reveal.

## Persistent consequences

The Day Four power failure and everything after it leaves persistent marks on
the ship, its people, and its systems. Damage is not reset; the world the
player evacuates is the world they learned.

## Later chord/song architecture

Beyond Episode One, subsequent chapters continue the chord and song
architecture: the chapters together build a larger piece of music, per the
locked rules document. Details of that architecture belong to future
authorities and remain reserved.

## Protection

This spine remains available to guide future implementation. No part of it may
be implemented until its gate is explicitly authorized by the creator. If an
approved requirement cannot be implemented faithfully when its time comes, the
conflict is flagged to the creator — never silently substituted.
