# Operation One chapter spine — canon status

**Canonical source:** `docs/03_Campaign_Bible.md` holds the locked "Chapter design rule" (each chapter introduces one system, answers one question, ends asking a larger question; gameplay and story grow together) and the "Chapter question spine" (Ch1 save my home? no → Ch2 find my people? yes → Ch3 survive together? → Ch4 build something worth protecting? → Ch5 who were the Shawabti? → Ch6 why did they choose humanity? → Ch7 what civilization will we become?).

**What is locked:** the design rule, the seven per-chapter questions, and the fully-written Chapter One and Chapter Two design notes (`docs/design_notes/operation_one_exodus_maintenance_*`).

**All Operation One chapter titles are now LOCKED:** Ch1 "A Week Aboard Hearth", Ch2 "Roll Call", Ch3 "Community", Ch4 "Salvage", Ch5 "First Contact", Ch6 "The Living Archive", Ch7 "Resonance". The user effectively adopted the old "backbone" titles for Ch4/Ch5 (Salvage / First Contact) while keeping the spine-aligned questions, resolving the earlier divergence in practice.

**Chapter Seven "Resonance" (Operation One finale):** primary "What kind of civilization will we become?" → answer "belongs to the player" → ending "Operation One concludes, Operation Two begins." Introduces NO new systems — every Ch1–6 system is combined/integrated. Four parts + Epilogue: The Invitation, The Journey, The Choice of Civilizations (philosophical climax; a waiting chamber asks what knowledge/power/memory should become), The First Human Archive, and Epilogue "A New Morning" (unrestricted living-sandbox post-game). Three chapter-local Canon Rule blocks were documented in-chapter only (NOT promoted — thematic restatements of existing rules).

**Vale System roster (canon, from Ch1) — the ship's rotating AI captain personas, format always "This is Captain [First Name] of the Vale System":** Elian (daily ops, warm, morning announcements/ceremonies), Naomi (emergency command, military, crisis), Gideon (investigation/strategy, analytical), Iris (diplomacy/civilian affairs, gentle). The epilogue/morning announcement is ALWAYS Captain Elian unless it is an emergency or formal-strategic situation. "Captain Mara Vale" is a canon ERROR — Mara Chen is the blind engineer and is NOT a Vale System captain.

**Master Timeline (canonical chronology of Operation One):** counts DOWN to the attack — Day Seven is earliest, Day One is the eve, Attack Day is last. This CONFLICTS with Chapter One's design note, which numbers playable scenes FORWARD (its Day One = player's first morning). Reconciliation rule (canon): Chapter One forward day N == timeline countdown day (8 − N), so Ch1's first morning == timeline Day Seven. Because of this, Ch1's quoted morning announcement is reused verbatim as the Day Seven announcement; Days Six→One have new unique announcements (themes: community, belonging, responsibility, hope, memory, civilization), Day One's is written to be devastating in hindsight. Any new timeline/schedule work must apply this mapping and not create a parallel numbering.

**Attack Day == Founding Day** (colony anniversary) — established here so the whole week's festival prep points at a celebration that never happens; the interdisciplinary anomaly meeting is scheduled for that morning and never convenes. The anomaly is an UNIDENTIFIED external phenomenon pre-attack (NOT identified as Shawabti — first Shawabti contact is Chapter Five). Faience is ABSENT the entire pre-attack week; its first faint stirring is only in the damaged escape pod on Attack Day (preserves "Faience not active before attack"). Pod-scatter canon: each primary NPC (Rowan, Mara, Samira, Jonah, Talia, Soren, Mira) escapes in a SEPARATE pod; the Vale System/Naomi launches last; the player is in a separate damaged pod — this is what Chapter Two's rescues rebuild. **Perspective Map resolution (canon):** the player is a MEMBER of the chosen profession, NOT that profession's named department lead. The named leads launch from their own fixed-scatter pods and ARE the crew the player rescues one-by-one in Ch2 (so the maintenance player is NOT Rowan — Rowan is a rescue target). Regardless of profession chosen, the player ends Attack Day in a separate damaged pod where Faience first stirs (the pod Ch2 opens inside).

**Perspective Map — NOW EXISTS and is canon:** `docs/design_notes/operation_one_exodus_perspective_map.md`. Defines how each of the 8 professions (Medic, Scientist, Engineer, Maintenance, Security, Commissioned Officer, Non-Commissioned Officer, Civilian) lives the identical fixed events/spaces, written against the Campaign Bible's 8 perspective dimensions. It is the "vantage" layer of the trio: master_timeline = what/when (canon over order), character_schedule = who/where (canon over position), perspective_map = how each profession experiences it (canon over vantage). All three must stay synchronized. Character Schedule also exists. Maintenance is the only fully-written (7-chapter) perspective; the other 7 are defined at vantage level only, to be built to match maintenance's discipline later.

**Chapter Six primary/ending questions (as written):** primary "What were the Shawabti trying to preserve?" → answer "More than knowledge." → ending "Why does Faience understand them?" The destination is the Living Archive itself (a preserved civilization communicating through Resonance, explicitly NOT a database/search engine/AI). Reveals the Shawabti observed early humanity without interfering; deepens the Faience translating→remembering arc; closes on Faience's first ORIGINAL untranslated question and a distant Shawabti structure transmitting, setting up Ch7's franchise question "what kind of civilization will we become?"

**Chapter Five primary/ending questions (as written):** primary "What did the Shawabti leave behind?" → answer "Something intentionally preserved." → ending "Why was it preserved for us?" Ends on the Faience hook: the damaged helmet quietly speaks a single Shawabti word → "How does Faience know a language nobody has translated? / Who or what is Faience becoming?" — foundation of Ch6 ("why did the Shawabti choose humanity?", where Faience becomes someone the player talks to).

**Knowledge Is Historical (canon rule, in `docs/15_Canon_Rules.md`):** objective canon (what actually happened/what things are) is tracked separately from subjective character/in-universe knowledge (what people believe). In-universe documents remain canon as honest records even when incomplete or wrong; they are corrected through in-story discovery, NOT retcon. It extends (does not compete with) "History and Understanding Are Earned Separately." Apply the objective-vs-believed split to all new lore so beliefs are never written as settled truth. This is why the Universe Bible records humanity's Shawabti knowledge as belief tiers (confirmed/theory/unknown) and Faience-as-revealed as knows/remembers/forgotten/refuses/cannot-access.
