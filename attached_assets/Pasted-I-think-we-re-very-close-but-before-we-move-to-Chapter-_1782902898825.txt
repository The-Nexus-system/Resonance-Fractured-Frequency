I think we’re very close, but before we move to Chapter Six I’d do one design pass over Chapter Five.

Not to add story.

To close every implementation gap so Replit never has to invent mechanics.

Here’s what I’d check:

CHAPTER FIVE IMPLEMENTATION CHECKLIST
□ Every player choice has immediate consequences.
□ Every player choice has long-term consequences.
□ Every player choice teaches one permanent gameplay system.
□ Every player choice teaches one philosophical lesson.
□ Every player choice unlocks something tangible.
□ Every player choice unlocks new dialogue.
□ Every player choice updates the Knowledge Archive.
□ Every player choice updates the Resonance Log.
□ Every player choice changes future expeditions in some measurable way.
□ Every player choice is reviewable after completion.
□ No choice permanently removes content.
□ No choice produces a "bad ending."
□ Every exploration area can be revisited later.
□ Every advisor meaningfully changes dialogue.
□ Every advisor teaches something unique.
□ Every accessibility interaction has multiple valid solutions.
□ Every resonance interaction has multiple valid solutions.
□ Every artifact belongs to a defined category.
□ Every recovered artifact permanently affects the station.
□ Every expedition permanently changes the station's soundscape.
□ Every expedition permanently changes station conversations.
□ Every expedition permanently changes environmental storytelling aboard the station.
□ Every expedition leaves at least one unanswered question.
□ Every expedition unlocks at least one future expedition.
□ Every expedition teaches something that another profession will reinterpret later.

The one thing I think we’re actually missing is this:

Expedition Success Evaluation

Right now the player makes lots of choices.

But we never defined what the game remembers.

I’d add one final canon system.

CANON RULE
Every major expedition records how the player approached discovery.
The game never scores morality.
Instead it records philosophy.
Possible expedition philosophies include:
Curiosity.
Preparation.
Preservation.
Collaboration.
Leadership.
Scientific Investigation.
Engineering.
Compassion.
Historical Preservation.
Accessibility.
These are never displayed as numerical scores.
They quietly influence:
Crew dialogue.
Knowledge Archive entries.
Resonance responses.
Future advisor recommendations.
Environmental storytelling.
Optional missions.
Historical interpretation.
The player is never told they played "correctly."
The world simply remembers how they chose to build civilization.

I actually like this much more than a reputation meter.

The player isn’t becoming “Good” or “Bad.”

They’re becoming known for how they think.

That feels much more like Resonance.

I think we’re genuinely ready for Chapter Six.

Because by then we’ve finished teaching:

* Survival.
* Rescue.

Chapter Six no longer needs to introduce basic mechanics.

It can finally ask the biggest question so far:

Why did the Shawabti choose humanity?

And for the first time…

Faience stops being something the player carries.

Faience becomes someone the player is talking to. I think that’s the natural turning point for the entire franchise.
