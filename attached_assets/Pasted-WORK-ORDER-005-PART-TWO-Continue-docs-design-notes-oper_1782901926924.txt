# Part Two

## The Threshold

### Purpose

Teach the player that exploration changes when entering another civilization.

Human technology solved problems.

Shawabti technology asks questions.

The player must slow down.

Observe.

Listen.

Learn.

The station cannot rush this discovery.

Neither should the player.

---

### Narrative Purpose

The structure appears untouched.

No blast damage.

No decay.

No emergency lighting.

No visible doors.

No obvious controls.

Nothing indicates it was abandoned.

It simply waited.

The signal originated from this structure.

As the player approaches, the repeating signal changes.

It begins responding to proximity.

Not randomly.

Intentionally.

The player realizes the structure is aware that someone has arrived.

Not who.

Simply that someone is present.

---

### Gameplay Purpose

Introduce:

Archaeological protocol.

Environmental observation.

Passive scanning.

Translation beginnings.

Resonance interaction.

Ethical exploration.

The player learns that entering unknown civilizations follows different rules than exploring human wreckage.

---

### Exploration Philosophy

The player is not invading.

The player is visiting.

Every interaction should begin with observation.

Not manipulation.

The game should reward patience.

Not speed.

---

### Environmental Storytelling

Before entering, the player may freely walk around the exterior.

Possible discoveries include:

Patterns carved into the structure.

Unknown symbols.

Weathering unlike any human material.

Ancient pathways.

Acoustic resonance when walking near certain walls.

Plant life adapting around the structure.

No visible entrances.

No defensive weapons.

No bodies.

No signs of violence.

Everything feels intentionally preserved.

---

### Player Decisions

Decision One

How should the player investigate?

---

Choice A

Perform a complete external survey.

Immediate Result

Additional structural information.

Detailed maps.

Future Effect

Interior navigation becomes easier.

Additional Archive entries unlock.

Exploration takes longer.

---

Choice B

Focus on the repeating signal.

Immediate Result

Signal analysis progresses faster.

Future Effect

Translation systems develop sooner.

Environmental understanding develops more slowly.

---

Choice C

Study surrounding environment first.

Immediate Result

Player discovers evidence of how the structure interacted with the surrounding landscape.

Future Effect

Additional environmental puzzles become easier later.

The player develops greater historical context.

---

Choice D

Approach the structure directly.

Immediate Result

Progress continues sooner.

Future Effect

Many optional discoveries remain unexplored until future expeditions.

Nothing is permanently lost.

---

### Advisor Contributions

Every advisor interprets the structure differently.

Rowan notices impossible construction tolerances.

He cannot identify seams.

He cannot explain how it was assembled.

Player learns:

Engineering sometimes begins with admitting what is not understood.

---

Mara notices the structure producing harmonic tones outside normal human engineering.

She asks the player to stop moving.

Simply listen.

Player learns:

Sound itself may be part of the architecture.

---

Samira notices something unexpected.

Nothing biological reacts with fear.

The surrounding ecosystem behaves normally.

Player learns:

Not every unknown is dangerous.

---

Jonah begins documenting everything.

He deliberately slows the investigation.

Player learns:

Good records prevent future mistakes.

---

Talia notices there are no defensive positions.

No obvious military planning.

Player learns:

Security can exist without visible weapons.

---

Soren becomes fascinated.

He refuses to form conclusions too early.

Player learns:

Observation always comes before theory.

---

Mira notices that the repeating symbols are not identical.

Adults assumed they were.

She finds subtle differences.

Player learns:

Patterns often contain variation.

Variation contains meaning.

---

The Vale System encourages the crew to compare observations before making decisions.

Player learns:

Leadership gathers perspectives before reaching conclusions.

---

### Gameplay Systems Introduced

Environmental archaeology.

Passive scanning.

Observation logs.

Pattern comparison.

Collaborative interpretation.

Ethical investigation.

---

### Accessibility

Every discovery must be available through multiple methods.

Visual observation.

Environmental audio.

Descriptive narration.

Captions.

Braille displays.

Tactile feedback.

No puzzle should require only vision.

No puzzle should require only hearing.

Every important environmental clue should exist through multiple sensory channels.

---

### Emotional Goal

The player should begin feeling awe rather than excitement.

Everything here feels older than history.

Yet somehow...

It feels welcoming.

Not abandoned.

Waiting.

---

### End Condition

After sufficient observation...

The repeating signal changes once more.

For the first time...

The structure answers the player.

Not with words.

Not with lights.

With resonance.

The harmonic tones produced by the structure slowly align with one another.

The player realizes something extraordinary.

The structure is not opening because they solved a puzzle.

It is opening because they took the time to observe before acting.

A previously invisible doorway gradually becomes accessible.

No alarms sound.

No dramatic music plays.

The structure simply welcomes the player inside.
