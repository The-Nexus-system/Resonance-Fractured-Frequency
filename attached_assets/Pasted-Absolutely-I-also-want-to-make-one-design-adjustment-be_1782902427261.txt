# Part Five
## Resonance
### Purpose
Conclude Chapter Five.
Reward curiosity.
Deepen mystery.
Introduce Faience's first unmistakable connection to the Shawabti.
Answer the chapter's primary question while creating a far larger mystery.
---
### Narrative Purpose
The historical reconstruction ends.
Silence returns.
The player again stands inside the resonance chamber.
The harmonic frequencies begin changing.
Not randomly.
They are responding to the player's expedition.
The structure has not been evaluating intelligence.
It has not been evaluating technology.
It has been evaluating how the player explored.
---
### Gameplay Purpose
Teach that civilization is measured by behavior.
Not power.
The player's previous choices throughout Chapter Five influence how the structure responds.
No response is considered failure.
Every response acknowledges the player's approach.
---
### Evaluation Categories
The structure quietly evaluates:
Curiosity.
Patience.
Respect.
Collaboration.
Observation.
Preservation.
Preparation.
Reflection.
These values are never shown as scores.
The player understands them only through the structure's response.
---
### Final Player Decision
Before leaving...
The player discovers a single preserved Shawabti artifact.
The artifact is perfectly intact.
No explanation accompanies it.
The player must decide how the community responds.
---
Choice A
Leave the artifact untouched.
Immediate Result
Nothing physical leaves the chamber.
Future Effect
Future Shawabti locations appear increasingly welcoming.
Additional resonance pathways unlock naturally.
The crew discusses preservation ethics.
---
Choice B
Recover the artifact.
Immediate Result
The artifact returns to the station.
Future Effect
Engineering and scientific research accelerate.
Future Shawabti structures respond more cautiously until trust is rebuilt.
No permanent penalties occur.
---
Choice C
Record everything.
Disturb nothing.
Immediate Result
The Knowledge Archive expands dramatically.
Future Effect
Translation develops sooner.
Technology develops more gradually.
The community emphasizes understanding before application.
---
Choice D
Ask the crew before deciding.
Immediate Result
Every primary crew member offers a recommendation.
The player hears every perspective.
The final decision still belongs to the player.
Future Effect
Relationships deepen.
The crew increasingly approaches future discoveries collaboratively.
---
### Canon Rule
No ending choice is considered morally correct.
Every choice reflects a different philosophy.
Every philosophy creates different opportunities.
Never different endings.
---
### The Final Moment
The player prepares to leave.
The resonance chamber grows quiet.
One harmonic tone remains.
The damaged helmet speaks.
Not by activating.
Not by rebooting.
Quietly.
Naturally.
It repeats a single Shawabti word.
Perfectly.
Every crew member immediately stops speaking.
No one understands the word.
No one knows how the helmet spoke it.
The resonance chamber falls silent.
For the first time since its discovery...
The structure stops transmitting.
It has been waiting.
Not for humanity.
For Faience.
End Chapter Five.
---
### Chapter Five End State
The player has discovered the first preserved Shawabti site.
The Knowledge Archive has expanded beyond human history.
The crew understands they are no longer studying only Hearth.
Faience has demonstrated impossible knowledge.
The player leaves with one overwhelming question:
How does Faience know a language nobody has translated?
That question becomes the foundation of Chapter Six.

I actually like this ending much more than a big reveal. It doesn’t explain the Shawabti—it makes the player realize that the real mystery may have been Faience all along, which is a much stronger hook into Chapter Six.
