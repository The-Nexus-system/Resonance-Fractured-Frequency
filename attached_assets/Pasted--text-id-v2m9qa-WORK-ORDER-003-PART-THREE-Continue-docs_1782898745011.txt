# Part Three

## Building Together

### Purpose

Teach collaborative construction.

Teach that no major project is completed by a single person.

Teach the player how individual expertise combines into community.

The player should never feel like they are simply pressing a build button.

Construction is a social activity.

---

### Narrative Purpose

The station begins physically changing because of decisions made during the community meeting.

Construction should happen gradually.

The player witnesses progress over time.

Rooms slowly evolve.

Equipment appears.

Hallways expand.

Temporary solutions become permanent.

The station grows because people are working.

---

### Gameplay Purpose

Introduce:

Collaborative construction.

Large-scale fabrication.

Multi-stage projects.

Project dependencies.

Resource logistics.

Construction planning.

Crew coordination.

Station evolution.

---

### Construction Philosophy

Every major construction project requires multiple crew members.

No single crew member can complete a major project alone.

Every completed project should visibly and audibly demonstrate cooperation.

The player learns that expertise overlaps.

No profession exists in isolation.

---

### Player Choice

The player reports to Rowan to begin the first community project.

Rowan presents three possible starting tasks based on the priority selected during the community meeting.

The player chooses which task to begin first.

Each task teaches different mechanics.

All tasks eventually become required.

The order changes the player's experience.

Canon remains unchanged.

---

### Collaborative Gameplay

Every construction project includes moments where the player assists different crew members.

These are not identical mini-games.

Each crew member teaches their own approach.

Examples:

Rowan teaches fabrication and precision.

Mara teaches listening for system health.

Samira teaches preparation before action.

Jonah teaches organization before construction.

Talia teaches identifying hazards before work begins.

Soren teaches documenting discoveries while building.

Mira notices practical improvements adults overlooked.

The Vale System helps resolve disagreements when multiple good solutions exist.

---

### Meaningful Decisions

Construction frequently presents choices.

Example:

A corridor can be built more quickly using salvaged materials.

Or

The player can spend additional time reinforcing it.

Quick Construction

Immediate Benefit

Project finishes sooner.

Future Consequence

Requires more maintenance later.

Reinforced Construction

Immediate Cost

Construction takes longer.

Future Benefit

Reduced maintenance.

Greater safety.

Example:

Install permanent accessibility features now.

Or

Delay installation until resources improve.

Immediate Installation

Higher initial resource cost.

Earlier accessibility benefits.

Additional optional interactions become available immediately.

Delayed Installation

Resources remain available for other projects.

Accessibility improvements arrive later.

No choice is considered morally correct.

Every decision changes how the station develops.

---

### Environmental Progression

Construction should never happen instantly.

Players should hear:

Metal cutting.

Tools.

Conversation.

Planning.

Laughter.

Occasional disagreement.

Power cycling.

Fabrication equipment.

The player should revisit completed areas and notice:

Fresh paint.

New lighting.

Additional signage.

Improved navigation.

Artwork.

Plants.

Personal belongings.

The station becomes increasingly personalized.

---

### Accessibility

Construction automatically considers accessibility.

The player never has to "remember" to build an accessible station.

Instead the player chooses:

Which accessibility improvements receive priority.

Examples include:

Improved tactile navigation.

Additional caption displays.

Braille station maps.

Adaptive workstations.

Environmental beacon improvements.

Wearable haptic support.

AAC communication terminals.

Every accessibility improvement benefits the entire community.

Not only disabled crew members.

---

### Gameplay Systems Introduced

Construction phases.

Project quality.

Collaborative building.

Accessibility planning.

Long-term infrastructure.

Environmental customization.

Station evolution.

---

### Emotional Goal

The player should understand:

They are no longer repairing wreckage.

They are helping build a civilization.

Every completed project should make the station feel more like a home than a survival shelter.

---

### End Condition

The first major community project reaches completion.

The player walks through the newly completed area.

Crew members naturally begin using it.

No celebration is announced.

Instead the player quietly witnesses ordinary life immediately filling the new space.

Someone sits down with coffee.

Someone starts working.

Someone laughs.

Mira immediately finds a new corner to explore.

Only after the player has experienced the completed space being lived in does the game acknowledge that construction is complete.

The next project is added to the station project board, leading into Part Four.
