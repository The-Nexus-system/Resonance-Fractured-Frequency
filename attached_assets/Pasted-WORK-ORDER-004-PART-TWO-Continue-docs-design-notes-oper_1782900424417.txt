# Part Two

## Departure

### Purpose

Teach the player how expeditions actually begin.

Leaving the station should always feel meaningful.

The player is leaving the safety of home.

The community watches them go.

Every departure should reinforce that someone is waiting for the player to return.

---

### Narrative

Construction briefly pauses.

Crew members naturally gather near the docking bay.

No ceremony is held.

This has already begun to feel normal.

The player performs a final systems check.

The advisor selected during mission preparation joins the player over radio.

The remaining crew continue supporting the expedition from home.

The player launches.

The station slowly disappears behind them.

---

### Gameplay Purpose

Teach:

Launch procedures.

Long-range navigation.

Mission logging.

Return point management.

Safe travel.

Orientation outside the station.

Players should learn that traveling away from home requires preparation.

---

### Sound Design

Before launch:

Airlocks cycling.

Dock clamps releasing.

Crew conversations fading.

Morning activity continues behind the player.

During departure:

Thrusters.

Navigation tones.

Beacon confirmation.

Station communications.

As distance increases:

The station grows quieter.

The surrounding debris field grows louder.

The player realizes how isolated they truly are.

---

### Accessibility

At any point the player may request:

Current heading.

Distance from home.

Estimated travel time.

Remaining oxygen.

Power status.

Mission objective.

Current destination.

Orientation information is always available without interrupting gameplay.

Blind players should never become disoriented while traveling.

---

### Player Decision

The player must choose the route toward Hearth.

The destination remains the same.

The journey differs.

--------------------------------------------------

Choice One

Shortest Route

Benefits

Arrive more quickly.

Consume fewer supplies.

Future Effects

Miss optional discoveries.

Fewer opportunities to recover minor salvage.

Lower Knowledge Archive growth.

---

Choice Two

Survey Route

Benefits

Map surrounding debris.

Identify future exploration locations.

Discover additional radio signals.

Future Effects

Additional optional expeditions unlock later.

Knowledge Archive expands.

Travel takes longer.

---

Choice Three

Hazard Route

Benefits

Travel through a dangerous debris field containing unusually valuable salvage.

Future Effects

Rare engineering materials.

Earlier advanced upgrades.

Greater navigation difficulty.

Higher equipment wear.

---

Choice Four

Historical Route

Benefits

Travel through known civilian sections of the former station.

Recover cultural information.

Hear preserved environmental recordings.

Future Effects

Additional historical discoveries.

Personal stories.

Family recordings.

Educational archives.

Unique conversations become available later.

Engineering rewards are reduced.

---

### Advisor Interaction

The selected advisor actively changes the journey.

Rowan notices structural debris.

Mara identifies damaged systems through sound.

Samira monitors environmental health.

Jonah tracks mission efficiency.

Talia identifies hazards.

Soren recognizes unusual scientific patterns.

Mira notices details adults overlook.

The Vale System provides balanced strategic guidance.

The advisor is not passive narration.

They actively teach the player throughout travel.

---

### Dynamic Events

During travel several optional discoveries may appear.

Examples include:

A drifting toolbox.

A damaged maintenance drone.

A personal backpack.

An emergency beacon.

A sealed cargo container.

A classroom assignment floating through space.

A family photograph.

A musical instrument.

The player chooses whether to investigate.

Ignoring these discoveries never prevents progression.

Investigating them expands understanding of Hearth and its people.

---

### Gameplay Systems Introduced

Long-range navigation.

Exploration routing.

Dynamic discoveries.

Mission logging.

Advisor dialogue.

Environmental storytelling.

---

### Emotional Goal

The player should gradually realize they are not simply traveling through wreckage.

They are traveling through the remains of people's lives.

Every object once belonged somewhere.

Every location once mattered to someone.

---

### End Condition

The player reaches the outer edge of Hearth's debris field.

The navigation computer identifies multiple possible expedition sites.

The player stops.

Looks outward.

For the first time since Chapter One...

The player voluntarily returns home.

Not to reclaim it.

To remember it.

The first exploration zone becomes available.

Part Three begins.
