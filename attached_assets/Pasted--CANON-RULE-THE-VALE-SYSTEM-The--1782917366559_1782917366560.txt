----------------------------------------------------------------

CANON RULE — THE VALE SYSTEM

The Vale System consists of one shared body occupied by four recurring system members.

The body itself is constant.

The identity being expressed is not.

The player gradually learns to recognize who is fronting through observation rather than explicit explanation.

The game should trust the player to notice recurring patterns.

----------------------------------------------------------------

SHARED CHARACTERISTICS

Every member shares:

• The same body.

• Height.

• Build.

• Face.

• Hair.

• Eye color.

• Skin tone.

• Physical scars.

• Medical history.

• Accessibility equipment.

• Uniform design.

• Rank insignia.

• Department affiliation.

The body never transforms.

Only its presentation changes.

----------------------------------------------------------------

UNIQUE PRESENTATION

Every member possesses a distinct personal identity.

Each member has their own:

• Voice.

• Speech rhythm.

• Vocabulary.

• Accent.

• Cadence.

• Breathing pattern.

• Walking rhythm.

• Standing posture.

• Head position.

• Eye contact.

• Facial expression.

• Resting posture.

• Hand placement.

• Gesture vocabulary.

• Preferred stance.

• Emotional expression.

• Leadership philosophy.

• Decision-making style.

• Priorities.

• Sense of humor.

• Professional strengths.

• Relationships with crew.

• Favorite locations aboard Hearth.

• Favorite routines.

• Preferred communication style.

No two members should ever feel interchangeable.

Players should eventually recognize each member before they speak.

----------------------------------------------------------------

UNIFORM IDENTITY

The Vale System wears one command uniform.

The uniform itself remains the same.

However, personal identifiers change naturally with whoever is fronting.

These include:

• Name tape.

• Digital identification badge.

• Wrist display.

• Command terminal login.

• Personal office display.

• Voice authentication.

• Personal communication profile.

When a switch occurs, these identifiers update automatically as part of normal ship operations.

Crew members treat these changes as completely ordinary.

Nobody comments on them unless it is directly relevant to the conversation.

----------------------------------------------------------------

FRONTING IS GAMEPLAY

The Vale System does not switch for dramatic effect.

Fronting occurs because another member is naturally better suited to the current situation.

Different members naturally take the lead during:

• Community leadership.

• Emergency command.

• Engineering coordination.

• Diplomatic discussions.

• Scientific planning.

• Civilian support.

• Crisis management.

• Long-term strategy.

The game rarely announces a switch.

Instead, players recognize it through:

• Different voice.

• Different posture.

• Different gait.

• Different command style.

• Different vocabulary.

• Different humor.

• Different emotional priorities.

• Different body language.

• Updated name tape.

• Updated command authentication.

• Different interactions with crew members.

The game rewards careful observation.

The player gradually comes to know each member as an individual while understanding they all share the same body.

Recognition emerges naturally through lived experience rather than exposition.
