# Part Four

## Homecoming

### Purpose

Complete the expedition gameplay loop.

Teach that returning home is as important as leaving it.

Demonstrate that discoveries only become meaningful when they are shared with the community.

Every expedition should permanently change the station.

Every expedition should permanently change the player.

Every expedition should permanently change the crew.

This becomes the standard ending structure for every major expedition throughout Resonance.

---

## Narrative Purpose

The player docks with the station.

The station immediately feels different from the silence of Hearth.

People are living.

Working.

Laughing.

Building.

The contrast between Hearth and home should be emotionally obvious.

The station is no longer simply where the player lives.

It is where the player belongs.

---

## Gameplay Purpose

Introduce:

Mission debriefing.

Salvage processing.

Artifact cataloging.

Knowledge Archive integration.

Community reactions.

Project unlocking.

Expedition reflection.

Station growth.

Future mission planning.

---

## Arrival

The player approaches the station.

Automatic docking systems respond.

Crew members continue their daily routines.

Nobody stops working simply because the player returned.

Instead...

People naturally gather as recovered materials begin arriving.

The player immediately understands that the expedition belongs to the whole community.

---

## Mission Debrief

The player attends a community debrief.

Every primary crew member responds according to their expertise.

Rowan evaluates recovered equipment.

Mara evaluates engineering systems.

Samira evaluates medical discoveries.

Jonah updates station inventories and future priorities.

Talia evaluates risks introduced by recovered materials.

Soren begins cataloging discoveries.

Mira asks questions nobody else considered.

The Vale System facilitates discussion.

The player participates.

The player does not simply watch.

---

## Player Decisions

Every expedition ends with four major decisions.

Every decision permanently affects station development.

Nothing permanently locks the player out of content.

Choices determine what develops first.

Not what develops forever.

--------------------------------------------------

Decision One

Recovered Equipment

Choice A

Install immediately.

Immediate Result

New gameplay systems become available immediately.

Future Effect

Resources are consumed.

Later upgrades require additional salvage.

--------------------------------------------------

Choice B

Study first.

Immediate Result

Knowledge Archive expands.

Research opportunities increase.

Future Effect

Installation takes longer.

Future upgrades become more efficient.

--------------------------------------------------

Choice C

Store for future use.

Immediate Result

Emergency reserve grows.

Future Effect

Current progress slows.

Future flexibility increases.

--------------------------------------------------

Decision Two

Recovered Historical Material

Choice A

Archive immediately.

Immediate Result

Knowledge Archive expands.

Additional conversations unlock.

Future Effect

Crew understanding grows.

New diplomatic dialogue becomes available in later chapters.

--------------------------------------------------

Choice B

Display throughout the station.

Immediate Result

Recovered artwork, photographs, educational materials and personal items appear throughout the station.

Future Effect

Crew morale improves.

The station increasingly reflects Hearth's culture.

Additional environmental storytelling develops.

--------------------------------------------------

Choice C

Return personal items to surviving families if possible.

Immediate Result

Items remain unavailable.

Future Effect

Later survivors recognize recovered belongings.

Relationships deepen.

Future missions gain additional emotional significance.

--------------------------------------------------

Decision Three

Recovered Resources

Choice A

Prioritize station expansion.

Immediate Result

Construction progresses rapidly.

Future Effect

Research and specialized equipment develop more slowly.

--------------------------------------------------

Choice B

Prioritize research.

Immediate Result

Knowledge Archive expands.

Future Effect

Construction progresses more gradually.

Additional discoveries become available sooner.

--------------------------------------------------

Choice C

Prioritize community wellbeing.

Immediate Result

Living spaces improve.

Accessibility improvements expand.

Crew morale increases.

Future Effect

Large construction projects require additional expeditions.

--------------------------------------------------

Decision Four

Next Expedition Priority

The player recommends what should receive attention next.

Engineering.

Medical.

History.

Infrastructure.

Unknown Signals.

The recommendation influences planning discussions at the beginning of the next expedition.

The player never becomes locked into one path.

The recommendation simply determines what the community prepares first.

---

## Community Changes

The station should visibly and audibly change after every expedition.

Examples include:

Recovered artwork hanging on walls.

Plants transplanted from Hearth.

Recovered books appearing in common areas.

Engineering equipment installed.

Medical devices activated.

Recovered music occasionally playing.

Children's educational materials displayed.

Crew members discussing discoveries while working.

Every expedition leaves permanent evidence throughout the station.

---

## Knowledge Archive

Every expedition updates the Knowledge Archive.

Entries include:

Recovered history.

Recovered engineering knowledge.

Recovered medical practices.

Recovered accessibility innovations.

Recovered cultural traditions.

Recovered personal stories.

Recovered scientific observations.

The player should immediately understand that preserving knowledge is just as valuable as recovering technology.

---

## Crew Conversations

Following every expedition, every primary crew member gains new optional conversations.

These conversations should reflect:

What was discovered.

How it changes their work.

How it changes their understanding of Hearth.

How it changes future plans.

The player is encouraged to spend time talking with the community before beginning another expedition.

---

## Accessibility

Every recovered accessibility innovation should become available to the player once it has been researched and installed.

Examples include:

Improved tactile navigation.

Expanded braille support.

Additional environmental beacon options.

Improved AAC interfaces.

Enhanced wearable haptic support.

Adaptive workstation improvements.

Accessibility evolves alongside civilization.

It is never treated as optional content.

---

## Emotional Goal

The player should feel relief upon returning home.

The station should become emotionally safer after every expedition.

Players should look forward to coming home just as much as leaving.

---

## Chapter Climax

As the community finishes cataloging the expedition...

Soren notices an anomaly.

The recovered data does not match known records.

Several engineering logs reference locations that should not exist.

Historical timestamps contain impossible inconsistencies.

The strange signal detected at the end of Chapter Three appears again.

This time...

It responds to something recovered during the expedition.

Not to the station.

Not to the player.

To something brought home.

The room falls silent.

Nobody understands why.

---

## Chapter End State

The expedition loop has been fully taught.

The player understands:

How to prepare.

How to explore.

How to recover discoveries.

How to return home.

How discoveries permanently change the station.

How community transforms exploration into progress.

The station is larger.

The crew is wiser.

The Knowledge Archive has expanded.

The mystery has deepened.

Chapter Four ends with one final question:

What was Hearth carrying that someone still wants?
