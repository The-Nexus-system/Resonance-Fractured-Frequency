You’re right. That’s the difference between a design document and an implementation document.

If we say:

“The player can choose who to visit.”

We have to answer:

* Why would they?
* What changes?
* What is learned?
* What is unlocked?
* What carries forward?

That’s the level Replit needs.

Here’s what I mean for Part One:

Player Choice Structure
The player wakes naturally.
There are no urgent objectives.
Instead, Jonah has left a morning schedule.
The player may visit the crew in any order.
The order chosen does not change canon.
The order changes what the player learns first, what conversations occur later in the day, and which optional gameplay bonuses become available earliest.
Every first visit permanently unlocks one gameplay concept.
The player is expected to eventually visit everyone before attending the community meeting.
No crew member should ever become optional.
--------------------------------------------------
Visit Rowan First
Player learns:
Preventative maintenance.
Rowan demonstrates inspecting systems before they fail.
Gameplay unlock:
Maintenance Inspection Mode.
The player can now perform preventative inspections around the station.
Benefits:
Reduced future equipment failures.
Small resource rewards.
Hidden maintenance problems become detectable.
Future dialogue:
Rowan later comments that the player is beginning to think like a maintenance engineer.
--------------------------------------------------
Visit Mara First
Player learns:
Active listening.
Mara teaches identifying system health entirely by sound.
Gameplay unlock:
Engineering Diagnostics.
The player begins recognizing different machinery states through their acoustic signatures.
Benefits:
Earlier warning of equipment failures.
Improved engineering puzzles.
Reduced repair time.
Future dialogue:
Mara begins occasionally asking the player what they hear before explaining the answer.
--------------------------------------------------
Visit Samira First
Player learns:
Communities survive because they prevent illness rather than simply treating it.
Gameplay unlock:
Crew Wellness.
The player can begin checking overall crew wellbeing instead of waiting for medical emergencies.
Benefits:
Improved morale.
Reduced long-term injuries.
Preventative medical projects become available.
Future dialogue:
Crew members begin voluntarily discussing health concerns with the player.
--------------------------------------------------
Visit Jonah First
Player learns:
Planning saves time.
Gameplay unlock:
Mission Planning Board.
The player can organize projects, prioritize work and schedule future tasks.
Benefits:
Projects complete more efficiently.
Reduced idle time.
Additional cooperative projects become available.
Future dialogue:
Jonah increasingly trusts the player's organizational ability.
--------------------------------------------------
Visit Talia First
Player learns:
Situational awareness prevents danger.
Gameplay unlock:
Environmental Risk Assessment.
The player can identify potential hazards before beginning projects.
Benefits:
Fewer accidents.
Safer exploration.
Improved rescue planning.
Future dialogue:
Talia begins involving the player in security planning rather than only emergency response.
--------------------------------------------------
Visit Soren First
Player learns:
Questions are as valuable as answers.
Gameplay unlock:
Research Notebook.
Players can now record theories, unanswered questions and observations for later investigation.
Benefits:
Knowledge Archive discoveries become easier to connect.
Hidden research opportunities become visible.
Future dialogue:
Soren begins asking the player for observations before giving explanations.
--------------------------------------------------
Visit Mira First
Player learns:
Curiosity reveals details expertise often overlooks.
Gameplay unlock:
Curiosity Prompts.
The player occasionally notices optional environmental details that begin side conversations, research opportunities and accessibility improvements.
Benefits:
Additional Knowledge Archive entries.
Optional discoveries.
Unique conversations.
Future dialogue:
Mira regularly asks the player questions that encourage exploration instead of objective chasing.
--------------------------------------------------
Visit the Vale System First
Player learns:
Leadership begins by listening.
Gameplay unlock:
Crew Consultation.
Major decisions now display who wishes to speak before the player commits to a course of action.
Benefits:
Better informed decisions.
Additional dialogue.
Improved diplomatic outcomes later in the campaign.
Future dialogue:
Members of the Vale System begin asking the player what they think before offering their own opinions.
--------------------------------------------------
Completion Requirement
Before the community meeting begins, the player must have visited every primary crew member at least once.
Every first conversation permanently unlocks one gameplay system.
Every first conversation permanently expands the player's understanding of how communities function.
The order changes the player's experience.
The destination does not.
Canon remains unchanged.

I actually think this is the structure we should use for almost every chapter going forward.

Every major section should answer four questions:

1. What decision does the player make?
2. What gameplay system is learned?
3. What philosophy or life lesson is learned?
4. What permanently changes because of that decision?

That gives Replit something concrete to build instead of just atmosphere, while keeping player choice meaningful even when the main story stays on the same timeline.
